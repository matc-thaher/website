    <h1>Mohammad Abu Thaher Chowdhury</h1>

    <p>
      I am a PhD student in Physics at
      <a href="https://www.rpi.edu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">
        Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute
      </a>
      in Troy, New York. My work lies at the intersection of theoretical and
      computational astrophysics, with a focus on dark matter, cosmology,
      galaxy formation, and galaxy evolution.
    </p>

    <p>
      My current research examines fuzzy dark matter as a theoretically
      motivated alternative to standard cold dark matter on small cosmological
      scales. I am interested in how the wave-like properties of ultralight
      bosonic dark matter affect the formation and internal structure of halos,
      particularly in the dwarf-galaxy mass regime. This work addresses open
      questions in small-scale structure formation, including the cusp-core,
      missing-satellites, and too-big-to-fail problems.
    </p>

    <p>
      I investigate these questions through cosmological simulations and
      numerical analysis. My workflow includes FDM simulations with GAMER-2,
      N-body comparison simulations with GIZMO, initial conditions generated
      with MUSIC and axionCAMB, and halo identification and characterization
      with Rockstar. I also develop open-source scientific software, including
      PHANTOM, a MATLAB and GNU Octave package that connects linear field
      statistics to dark matter halo observables.
    </p>

    <p>
      Before beginning my PhD, I earned an M.S. in Physics from the
    `  <a href="https://www.utrgv.edu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">
        University of Texas Rio Grande Valley
      </a>
      in 2022. There, I worked with
      <a href="https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=C2xWWFsAAAAJ&hl=en"
         target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">
      Professor Soumya D. Mohanty
    </a>
      on gravitational-wave data analysis, with particular attention to the
      identification, estimation, and subtraction of transient non-astrophysical
      noise artifacts in detector data. My master's thesis,
      <em>"Glitch Estimation and Removal Using Adaptive Spline Fitting and
      Wavelet Shrinkage on the Gravitational Wave Data"</em>, is available through
      the
      <a href="https://www.proquest.com/docview/2801919094?pq-origsite=gscholar&fromopenview=true&sourcetype=Dissertations%20%26%20Theses"
         target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">
        ProQuest eTheses Repository
      </a>.
    </p>

    <p>
      I earned a B.Sc. in Physics from
      <a href="https://www.sust.edu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">
        Shahjalal University of Science and Technology
      </a>
      in Sylhet, Bangladesh, in 2015, followed by an M.S. in Physics from SUST
      in 2017. During my master's work, I studied condensed-matter physics,
      with a focus on perovskite conductivity.
    </p>

    <h2>Research and Technical Interests</h2>

    <p>
      My research interests include theoretical cosmology, dark matter
      phenomenology, structure formation, galaxy formation and evolution,
      gravitational-wave data analysis, and fundamental physics. I am
      particularly interested in the theoretical relationship between quantum
      phenomena, gravitation, cosmology, and the large-scale behavior of the
      universe.
    </p>

    <p>
      I am also interested in computational physics, numerical methods,
      scientific software development, signal processing, simulation-based data
      analysis, and reproducible research workflows. These interests include
      quantum computing, especially the possibility of applying quantum
      algorithms to astrophysical simulations and scientific data-analysis
      problems. My technical work connects theoretical questions in physics
      with simulation, software, and data-intensive methods.
    </p>
